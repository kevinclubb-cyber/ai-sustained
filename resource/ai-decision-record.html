One process · One page · Four gates

The AI Adoption Decision Record

Fill one in per process, before anybody talks about headcount. If you cannot complete gate two with real numbers, you have not finished thinking.

No gate is passed on a forecast. Only on a measurement.

01 Map

Can somebody confirm the output is right without redoing the work?

This is the whole test. Where checking is cheap and already somebody's job, the gain survives. Where checking means rebuilding the reasoning, it costs roughly what doing it cost, and the saving disappears. It predicts which functions reverse better than any list of automatable tasks.

02 Measure

Run it for one month and count four things.

Not a pilot on tidy data. A month of real work, including the awkward inputs. The fourth counter is the difficult one and the only one that catches cost moving rather than disappearing.

Every attempt, not every success
Needed a fresh go or a rewrite
Time spent checking, on average
By somebody other than the operator
03 Deploy

Give it to your least experienced people first.

Novice support agents gained 34%. Experienced agents gained close to nothing, and expert developers on their own code went 19% slower. Every study points the same way, and it is the opposite of most people's instinct. Your best people are the wrong pilot group.

04 Decide

Only now, and only on the numbers above.

Never on a self-reported time saving. The one study that measured both found people wrong by 43 points, in the optimistic direction, immediately after experiencing the opposite. Measure it, or drop the claim.

The arithmetic
Hours a week this used to take
Hours a week it takes now, doing it
Hours a week spent checking and re-running
Hours a week the mistakes cost somebody else
Real hours saved per week 0.0

Fill the four rows in. This is the number that goes on the slide, not the first one.

The two-year test

Klarna's loop from announcement to correction ran twelve to eighteen months. Gartner expects half of AI-driven cuts to be reversed by 2027, with rehires returning 20% to 35% above the salary that was cut. Push your payback out two years and add the cost of hiring them back.
