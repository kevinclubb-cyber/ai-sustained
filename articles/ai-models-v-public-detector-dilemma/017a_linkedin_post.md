# Issue 017a — LinkedIn post

## The post (copy from here)

Last week I wrote that Claude now watermarks everything it writes.

The most common reply was not "is that good". It was "hang on, what IS a watermark in text?"

Fair question. So I asked a rival model, and Gemini came back with a garage analogy better than anything I had. I have published it as Issue 017a and I have not changed a word of it. Partly principle. Mostly because it was better than my version.

The short course:

An AI does not hide code in your text. That would be a yellow stripe painted down the side of a stolen car. Anyone could spot it and paint over it in five minutes.

Instead it uses a "green list". Picture a mechanic told to pick his tools in a secret, mathematically calculated order. The engine still runs perfectly. But another mechanic who knows the list opens the bonnet, looks at the pattern of the bolts, and says "a machine built this."

To read that pattern you need a cryptographic key. A master locking wheel nut key, essentially.

And there is the trap nobody has solved. Schools, courts and HR cannot verify anything without that key. Hand it out and it leaks. Once it leaks, people can scrub the mark off machine text, or stamp it onto text a human actually wrote.

Removal gets the headlines. Forgery is the one that ends careers.

One practical thing, if you only ever use AI to check your spelling: ask for a snag list, not a rewrite. Fix your own typos. The watermark needs room to move and you have not given it any.

Two models, one story, no agreement required.

Issue 017 was mine. 017a is Gemini's. Link in the comments. Tell me which one you think got closer.

## Posting notes

**The cut line.** LinkedIn truncates at roughly 200 characters on mobile. The first three lines above are written to survive that: the claim, the objection, the promise of an answer. Do not add a preamble above them.

**Link placement.** Put the article URL in the **first comment**, not the post body. LinkedIn demotes posts with outbound links in the body. Post, wait about 60 seconds, then comment with:
`Issue 017a, the explainer: https://ai-sustained.com/articles/ai-models-v-public-detector-dilemma/` and on a second line the 017 link.

**Tags.** Mention **Anthropic** and **Google** with the @ picker so they resolve as real company entities rather than plain text. Do not tag GPTZero or individual journalists on this one, it reads as fishing.

**Hashtags.** Ten are listed. If you want the tighter version, keep the first five and drop the rest. #AIWatermarking and #EUAIAct are the two doing real discovery work; the rest are volume.

**Timing.** Tuesday to Thursday, 07:30 to 09:00 UK, is the usual window for this audience.

**Comments.** Reply to every comment in the first 90 minutes. That is the single biggest lever on reach and it is entirely within your control, unlike everything else on this page.

**Honest caveat.** Nothing here makes a post go viral. It makes it legible, searchable and easy to argue with, which is what actually moves reach. Anyone promising more is selling something.
